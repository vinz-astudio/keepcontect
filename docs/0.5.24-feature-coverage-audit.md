# Keep Contact 0.5.24 — production-to-prototype feature coverage audit

Date: 2026-08-02  
Scope: current React/Capacitor/Tauri source at 0.5.24 versus `Prototype/interactive_prototype.html`.

## Evidence boundary

This is a source and interaction-contract audit, not a fresh screenshot audit. The selected in-app Browser is showing a local `file://` page that the available Browser control cannot inspect, so visual spacing, clipping, and rendered-state claims are excluded. The audit can still establish which production data, states, and actions have or have not been carried into the prototype.

## Main conclusion

The prototype improves calmness, hierarchy, and plain-language setup, but it reduces the product's operational usefulness. The largest regression is Watch: the production app has alerts, escalation progress, acknowledgement actions, check-in tasks, monitored groups, per-member activity status, coverage/error states, and a Concern action. The prototype replaces most of that with two sample people and a three-item activity stream.

This is not an acceptable simplification unless every removed capability has an explicit destination. The redesign needs a coverage invariant: every production datum, state, and action must either appear in the prototype, move to a clearly reachable detail view, or be deliberately retired with a stated reason.

## People / Watch minimum information contract

The default screen should stay calm without becoming vague. Use progressive disclosure:

1. **Action summary:** active alerts, overdue check-ins, and people whose evidence is limited/unknown.
2. **People list:** every watched person, ordered by urgency. The collapsed row shows name, relationship or circle, state, most recent trusted evidence age, evidence source class, and coverage quality.
3. **Person detail:** devices contributing evidence, recent evidence events, threshold/context explanation, monitoring direction, privacy restrictions, and available actions such as Concern.
4. **Tasks:** pending and overdue check-ins remain a separate actionable block.
5. **History:** the activity stream is chronological evidence and action history, not the primary status model.

Sensitive raw activity content is not required. “Phone activity”, “manual check-in”, “app heartbeat”, and “movement available” are sufficient source classes. The UI should explain evidence without exposing what the person did on their phone.

## Truthfulness defects that must be repaired

- `No active alerts` must not be followed by `Everything looks safe and sound`. The safe claim is “No active alerts right now”; coverage and unknown states remain visible beside it.
- `Live updates` must only appear when a real subscription or refresh channel is healthy.
- `Ready` must be based on capability-specific runtime evidence, not local button state.
- Prototype actions must be labelled as demo behavior unless they call the real service.
- SOS must not claim server acceptance or a named person's acknowledgement without receipts.
- Invite expiry, emergency-info access, background reliability, and multi-device readiness must match runtime behavior rather than aspirational copy.

## Why the earlier review missed the Watch regression

The earlier audit checked safety wording, privacy boundaries, platform honesty, navigation, and whether interactions could mislead. It did not build a field-by-field production-to-prototype coverage matrix. Existing static checks likewise assert semantics and accessibility, but not that every production datum/action still has a destination. That missing acceptance dimension allowed a visually cleaner Watch screen to pass even though its usable information and actions had been reduced.

The permanent correction is to require this coverage matrix for every redesign and to add tests for the destination of each P0/P1 production state and action.

## Recommended implementation order

1. Watch truth model, people density, alerts, and tasks.
2. SOS timing and truthful delivery states.
3. Onboarding runtime verification and per-capability readiness.
4. Circles, responsibility direction, privacy sharing, Guardians, and QR handoff.
5. Me account/device/update/push/pattern/emergency-info parity.
6. Routine save/error/consent parity and final cross-platform state verification.

Implementation should update the prototype as the testable UX contract and then apply the approved behavior to the production React/native surfaces. Visual polish alone is not completion.
